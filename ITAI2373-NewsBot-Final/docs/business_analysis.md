# Business case — executive evidence
## Value proposition and use cases
An editorial analyst can consolidate licensed articles, compare coverage, triage topics and export traceable reports; a policy analyst can follow topics by month and language; a campus researcher can reproduce an NLP pipeline without paid inference. These are proposed use cases, not claimed customer deployments.
## ROI scenario (illustrative, not measured)
Assume 2 analysts, 10 article-review hours per analyst per week, 20% time savings, 48 weeks/year, $30/hour loaded labor. Recovered time = 192 hours/year; gross value = $5,760/year. Assume $1,800 first-year implementation/training and $600 hosting/support = $2,400. Net first-year value = $3,360, ROI = 140%, simple payback = 5 months at $480/month gross value (cost treated as upfront for this scenario). Sensitivity: 10% savings → $2,880 gross, $480 net, 20% ROI; 30% → $8,640 gross, $6,240 net, 260% ROI. Validate time savings with timed human tasks before spending; no revenue or cost saving has been observed.
## Competitive comparison (qualitative capability positioning, not price quotes)
| Approach | Strength | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| NewsBot | local-first corpus, reproducible NLP, transparent source IDs and exports | no live licensed feed, production controls or external validation |
| Manual search/spreadsheets | human context, flexible, familiar | repetitive triage and inconsistent provenance |
| Commercial media monitoring | managed coverage, alerting and support | subscription/license terms and less control over internal processing |
| General-purpose LLM workflow | flexible natural-language synthesis | source grounding, privacy, costs and reproducibility require engineering |
## Acceptance criteria
Run a licensed multi-source pilot, compare review time and citation precision, benchmark language and class performance, track false positives, inspect operating costs and obtain editorial approval. No commercial product claims are based on unverified current pricing.
